LeetCode Premium vs Free Alternatives in 2026: Is Premium Worth It?

LeetCode Premium is one of those purchases that feels obvious and questionable at the same time. Obvious because everyone preparing for a technical interview seems to have it. Questionable because the free tier is already enormous—thousands of problems, a working code editor, discussions, and community solutions—so what exactly are you paying for?

The answer used to be simple: company tags. If you knew you were interviewing at Google, Premium let you filter problems by company. That single feature justified the cost for a lot of people. But the interview prep landscape has changed. Company tag data is less reliable than it used to be, AI assistants have made some Premium features redundant, and the free alternative ecosystem has matured considerably.

So the question in 2026 is genuinely worth asking: is Premium still worth it, or can you get the same outcome for free?

I've used both. I've coached people through interview prep with Premium and without it. The honest answer is that it depends on three things—your budget, your timeline, and how you learn best. This post breaks down exactly what Premium adds, what the free tier covers, and how the free alternatives compare. If you want the broader list of alternatives, our free LeetCode alternatives guide covers the full landscape.

What LeetCode Premium Actually Adds

Let's start with the concrete features. Premium (typically around $159/year or $35/month as of this writing, with regional pricing variations) adds these things on top of the free tier.

Company tags. This is the headline feature. Premium lets you filter problems by the companies that have asked them. It's useful context—if you're interviewing at a company that leans heavily on graph problems, the tags can help you prioritize.

But the tags have a caveat that's worth understanding. They're crowd-sourced from user reports, which means they reflect where problems have been seen, not where they'll be asked. A problem tagged "Google" in 2019 might still be tagged in 2026 because nobody un-tags old data. The signal is directional, not precise. Treat company tags as a hint, not a syllabus.

Premium-only problem sets. A portion of LeetCode's problem library is locked behind Premium. These are typically newer problems, problems with detailed company context, or problems that were moved from free to paid at some point. The exact count changes over time, but it's a meaningful chunk of the catalog.

Curated study plans. LeetCode's study plans—like the "Blind 75" derivative lists, the top interview questions, and the various topic-specific plans—are more fully featured on Premium. The free tier has some study plans, but the Premium ones are more comprehensive and include company-specific tracks.

Detailed solutions and video explanations. Free users see community solutions and the discussion tab. Premium adds official solutions with editorial-quality writeups, plus video explanations for a large portion of the problems.

The debugger. Premium includes an interactive debugger that lets you step through your code. For people who learn by tracing execution, this is genuinely useful. For people who debug with console.log (which is most of us), it's less essential.

Interview simulation. Premium offers a mock interview mode with timed sessions. It's a structured way to practice under pressure. It's also a fairly thin feature compared to what dedicated mock interview platforms offer.

Faster code execution. Premium users get priority in the code execution queue. In practice, this means shorter waits when you run submissions during peak hours. It's a quality-of-life improvement, not a learning feature.

No ads. The free tier shows ads. Premium doesn't. Whether that matters to you is personal.

What the Free Tier Actually Covers

The free tier of LeetCode is not a demo. It's a substantial platform.

The problem library. Thousands of problems are free, covering every topic that shows up in technical interviews: arrays, strings, trees, graphs, dynamic programming, design problems, database queries, and more. The vast majority of the "most asked" problems—the ones that show up in the Blind 75, the NeetCode 150, and similar lists—are free.

The code editor and test runner. Free users get the full editor, the ability to run code against test cases, and submission. There's no meaningful restriction on how much you can practice.

Community solutions and discussions. This is where the free tier's value really lives. The discussion tab on popular problems has multiple approaches, complexity analyses, and edge-case discussions from people who have solved the problem in different languages. It's often more useful than a single official solution because you see multiple perspectives.

Contests. LeetCode's weekly and biweekly contests are free. They're one of the best ways to practice under time pressure, and they're available to everyone.

Some study plans. The free tier includes access to several study plans and the daily problem.

The practical upshot: a motivated person can prepare for technical interviews entirely on the free tier. The gaps are real, but they're narrow.

The Free Alternatives That Actually Matter

The "free alternative to LeetCode Premium" question is really a question about which of Premium's specific features you can replicate elsewhere. Here's the honest mapping.

For curated problem lists: NeetCode

NeetCode is the closest thing to a free replacement for Premium's curated study plans. It organizes problems by pattern—two pointers, sliding window, backtracking, and so on—and the "NeetCode 150" list is widely regarded as one of the best structured preparation paths available. The problem solutions are on YouTube, free.

What it replaces: Premium's study plans and editorial solutions. What it doesn't replace: company tags, the integrated platform experience.

For company-specific prep: community-maintained lists

The "LeetCode company tagged questions" that people used to pay for are now widely mirrored in community repositories, GitHub gists, and various interview prep sites. They're less reliable than the live Premium data, but they're free and often good enough for directional prioritization.

What it replaces: the surface-level value of company tags. What it doesn't replace: the fresher, more complete Premium tag data.

For explanations and solutions: YouTube and discussion tabs

NeetCode, Abdul Bari, and a dozen other channels cover the most important problems with high-quality explanations. Combined with LeetCode's own discussion tab, the free solution ecosystem is arguably better than Premium's official solutions—more varied, more opinionated, and more reflective of how real people actually solve problems.

For mock interviews: Pramp and interviewing.io

Pramp offers free peer-to-peer mock interviews. interviewing.io has a free tier and paid sessions with real engineers. Both are more realistic than Premium's interview simulation because you're talking to an actual human, which is the part that interview simulation is supposed to practice.

For structured courses: free tiers of AlgoExpert, Educative, and similar

Several paid platforms offer free samples or limited free tiers. They're not full replacements, but they can fill specific gaps. Our free LeetCode alternatives guide covers the full list with a breakdown of what each one is actually good for.

The Case for Premium

Premium makes sense in these situations.

You have a short timeline and a specific target company. If you're interviewing at one company in three weeks, company tags plus curated plans plus official solutions is a compressed path to focused preparation. The time saved is worth more than the subscription cost.

You learn best from structured, authoritative material. If you find community solutions noisy and prefer official editorials with consistent quality, Premium delivers that. Some people do better with a single well-written solution than with fifteen conflicting discussion posts.

You value the integrated experience. Everything in one place—problems, solutions, company filters, mock interviews, the debugger—has real value. Context switching between LeetCode, YouTube, and a separate mock interview platform has a cost.

Your employer or bootcamp is paying. A surprising number of companies and programs reimburse LeetCode Premium. If someone else is paying, the calculus changes entirely.

The Case Against Premium

Premium is hard to justify in these situations.

You're early in your preparation. If you're still learning data structures and algorithms, the free tier has more than enough problems. Premium features like company tags are most useful when you're already interview-ready and narrowing down.

You're on a tight budget. $159 is not trivial, especially for students or career changers. The free tier plus NeetCode plus Pramp covers the vast majority of what Premium offers.

You're preparing broadly, not for one company. Company tags are the main differentiator, and they're most valuable when you're targeting a specific employer. For general preparation, the free tier and free alternatives are sufficient.

You use AI assistants for explanations. A lot of what Premium's official solutions provide—step-by-step explanations, complexity analysis, alternative approaches—is something a modern AI assistant can do for free. This is a newer development, but it's real. The official solutions are still higher quality than a generic AI response, but the gap has narrowed.

You've already solved the core lists. If you've worked through the Blind 75 or NeetCode 150, the marginal value of Premium's additional problems is lower. You're better off doing mock interviews than grinding more problems.

A Story About Buying Premium Too Early

A friend of mine bought LeetCode Premium in the first week of his interview prep. He was three months out from starting to apply, and he was still shaky on dynamic programming. He spent the first month filtering problems by company tags for a company he wasn't even sure he'd apply to, working through problems that were above his current level, and getting discouraged.

The Premium subscription wasn't the problem. The timing was. He was paying for precision targeting before he had the fundamentals to target.

About six weeks in, he stopped using the company filters and went back to NeetCode's pattern-based list. He worked through the fundamentals, got comfortable with the patterns, and then—about a month before he actually started applying—renewed Premium and used the company tags for the final push.

That sequence worked. Premium was valuable in the last month, and it was a distraction in the first. The lesson: Premium is a precision tool. Buy it when you need precision, not when you're still building the foundation.

Decision Framework

Here's how I'd think about it.

Buy Premium if:

  • You're interviewing at specific companies within the next 1–3 months.
  • You have the budget and the $159 isn't a meaningful expense.
  • You learn better from official, structured solutions than from community discussion.
  • You want everything in one place and value the integrated experience.

Skip Premium and use free alternatives if:

  • You're early in your preparation and still building fundamentals.
  • You're on a tight budget.
  • You're preparing broadly rather than targeting specific companies.
  • You're comfortable assembling your own prep path from NeetCode, YouTube, and Pramp.
  • You've already worked through the core problem lists.

The middle path: Start free. Work through a structured list like NeetCode 150. Do a few contests to gauge where you stand. Then, when you have actual interviews scheduled and you know which companies you're targeting, buy Premium for a month or two for the final push. The monthly option exists for exactly this reason—you don't have to commit to a year.

Wrapping Up

LeetCode Premium is a good product with a real use case, but it's not the default purchase that a lot of people treat it as. The free tier is substantial, the free alternative ecosystem is strong, and the main differentiator—company tags—is most valuable when you're already interview-ready and targeting specific employers.

Buy Premium when you need precision, not when you're still building the foundation. If you're on the fence, the free tier plus NeetCode plus Pramp will get you most of the way there. If you have interviews scheduled and the budget, Premium is a reasonable accelerant.
